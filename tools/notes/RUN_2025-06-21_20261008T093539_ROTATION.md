**Relocation** (`relocate_marks_clear_of()` line 282, `_tick_window()` line
241, `_placed()` line 269): a tick overlapping an annotation bbox moves along
its own axis to the nearest clear position inside a window that keeps a corner
tick in its half along and within the outer sixth across (`_CORNER_BAND` line
226) and a mid tick in the central 37.5–62.5 % band across (`_MID_BAND` line
227); the level may move within that band; a tick with no clear position stays
put (`blocked`). Moved positions are what the sidecar's `marks[*].uv0/uv1`
record, and what the prediction uses when a sidecar is on hand.

**The rendered polygon.** For a rectangle w × h rotated θ, the bounding box is
W = w cos θ + h sin θ, H = w sin θ + h cos θ; inverting, w = (W cos θ − H sin
θ) / cos 2θ, h = (H cos θ − W sin θ) / cos 2θ (`census_registration.inscribed_polygon`),
centred in the box, rotation direction chosen as the one holding more of the
ink hull's vertices (`rendered_polygon`). A tick is predicted inside when both
its endpoints lie inside the polygon on the nominal mapping (`ticks_inside`).

**Why the box's inset is the wrong place.** The polygon touches each box edge
at one point; away from it the polygon's boundary leaves the edge at tan θ
(the shallow edge) or cot θ (the steep one). At 12.9°, a tick 24 px inside the
edge is inside the polygon only within 24 / tan 12.9° ≈ 105 px of the tangent
point along the shallow edge, or 24 × tan 12.9° ≈ 5.5 px along the steep one.
The corners are never within that; a mid tick is only when the tangent point
falls near 0.4 of the edge, which for 3939979's aspect it does not.

### 6.4 Two ways to make the ticks render

**(a) Place the ticks inside the rendered polygon; keep the authored frame.**
Compute the largest axis-aligned rectangle inside the loop (for a rotated
rectangle it is closed-form; for a general loop, the largest inscribed
axis-aligned rectangle of a polygon) and hand THAT rectangle to
`registration_mark_segments()` as `reference_uv` (line 136 already takes any
rectangle; `relocate_marks_clear_of()` then works unchanged). The ticks stay
horizontal and vertical, so the locator (`tools/registration_marks.py`
`_locate`, thirds over the ticks' own extent) needs no change, and the fit's
UV ↔ pixel mapping is exact as before. The export is the authored frame: what
the sheet shows, inside the bounding box, white outside. **Occupancy then
represents the sheet's content on the model lattice; the denominator is still
the bounding box** (§5) until the analysis masks to the recorded loop, which
`tools/stage_a_grid.py` can do the way it masks to split bands (line 360:
`split_crop_bands`; the loop becomes a pixel mask instead of bands). The
inscribed rectangle is smaller than the loop, so the ticks' spread shrinks: for
3939979's geometry (3579 × 2991 px at 12.91°) the largest centred inscribed
axis-aligned rectangle is 3132 × 2348 px (computed on the measured polygon),
69 % of the loop's area; the fit's lever arm is that rectangle's extent, still
thousands of pixels.

**(b) Un-shape the crop inside the rolled-back group; render the box.**
`manager.RemoveCropRegionShape()` in its own Transaction after the loop is
recorded (the Q7 move, `remove_split_for_capture()` line 156), rolled back
with the group. The export is then the full bounding box, including content
the sheet never shows in its corners; the ticks are at the box's inset as
today and register as today. **Occupancy would then represent the box's
content, 44 % more area than the sheet shows on 3939979, and every annotation
element the author placed outside the rotated crop but inside its box would be
counted as the sheet's** — unless the analysis masks to the recorded loop,
which (b) needs just as much as (a). It also changes what Revit draws
(D, 2026-10-01: a crop write that read back identical changed 8.8 % of a split
elevation's pixels), so it needs the probe below run both ways and the pixels
inside the loop compared.

**Recommendation:** (a), because it captures what the sheet shows and leaves
the authored crop untouched; (b) only as a probe control.

### 6.6 Finding the views across runs, and recapturing them in place

**Finder (built, read-only):** `tools/find_recapture_views.py <run> … --out <dir>`
runs the sidecar half of the census on each run (no TIFF read; seconds per
run) and classifies every one of the run's own views: `recapture` when the
registration was refused with fewer ticks found than drawn, or ticks were
drawn and no record exists; `review` when refused with every tick found (a
residual, as 929475) or the record does not match its files; `keep` when
registered. It writes `recapture_views.csv` and `recapture_lists.json`, the
latter per run: `run_dir`, `run_id`, `as_of_date`, `recapture: [ids]`,
`review: [ids]`. On 06-21 (OBSERVED,
`tools/notes/data/recapture_lists_2025-06-21.json`): recapture 52, review 1
(929475), keep 102; on the eleven 06-13 G2–G4 captures: keep 11. Over the 43
runs it is one command per export root (the roll-up's `@runs` form) and
answers "which dates" without opening Revit.

**Recapture in place (BUILT: `vop_interwoven/recapture.py`, the streaming
exporter's `recapture=` parameter, `thinrunner_streaming.py` IN[7];
`tests/test_recapture.py`, 27 tests at three levels — pure, real exporter and
entry point with the capture faked, real registered capture on the fake
world). Where the build differs from the proposal below: the sidecar record
carries `replaces_exporter_version` (the run's build) rather than the old
sidecars' sha256 — the census already hashes the files it reads, and a
recapture that was refused leaves them untouched; `views_timing_*` is an
analysis-layer file (`tools/stage_a_timing_report.py`), not the exporter's;
`views_diagnostics` and the view-element map need no merge mode, both merge
per view under the run's id already; one recapture written in chunks (the
thinrunner's batches) is ONE `recaptures[]` entry, found by the first version
of its test; and a REFUSED view keeps its old views_core row and run_meta
entry too, not only its files, listed under `recaptures[].refused_view_ids`.**
What the capture needs so a listed view can be captured again INTO its
original run, replacing that view's files and rows, and so the analysis knows
it was:

1. **Input.** `thinrunner_streaming.py` takes the finder's
   `recapture_lists.json` (a new IN slot) instead of a view list. For each
   run entry it refuses unless the open document's title equals the run's
   `run_meta.doc_title` (the recapture must come from the same archive; the
   as-of date stays the archive's), and unless every id is in that run's
   `views_requested`.
2. **Identity.** The run directory is the existing one. `check_run_directory`
   (`run_meta.py:158`) is called with `continuing=True` and the run_id read
   from the existing `run_meta.json` (today `thinrunner_streaming.py:539–542`
   mints a new id and refuses a used directory; a recapture continues the
   run it patches). Every new sidecar carries the original `run_id` plus a
   `recapture` record: `{"captured_at": <execution clock id>,
   "exporter_version": <this build>, "reason": "ticks_outside_crop_loop",
   "replaces": {"model_sidecar_sha256": …, "anno_sidecar_sha256": …}}`.
   `run_meta.json` keeps its `exporter_version` and gains
   `recaptures: [{captured_at, exporter_version, view_ids}]`, and each
   recaptured entry of `views[]` gains the same `recapture` key, so a reader
   can tell which views of a run come from which build.
3. **Guard before overwriting.** With §6.1–6.3 in, the capture predicts
   whether every tick lies inside the crop loop BEFORE the export; a
   recapture that would again leave ticks outside refuses that view and
   leaves its files as they are (a recapture that reproduces the failure
   must not replace a capture with one that looks the same).
4. **Files.** `<stem>.json`, `.tiff`, `_anno.json`, `_anno.tiff` are
   rewritten for the listed views only; the stale `_anno.registered.json`,
   `.grid.*`, `.kinds.*` are left for the analysis tools to overwrite
   (`register_stage_a_annotation.py` and `stage_a_grid.py` already write
   per view; re-running them over the run is idempotent for the untouched
   views, and the roll-up is a full rewrite by design). The run's CSVs
   (`views_core`, `views_diagnostics`, `views_timing`,
   `vop_view_element_map`) get their rows for the listed views replaced,
   keyed by `(RunId, ViewId)` — today they are written per run or appended
   per batch, so this is a merge mode in the streaming exporter's writers.
5. **Analysis impact: breaking.** A run then holds captures from two
   builds; `run_meta.exporter_version` no longer describes every view, and
   the per-view `recapture` record is what the analysis layer compares on.
   The census and the roll-up should surface it (`recaptured_by` column)
   so a BI model does not mix the two without knowing.
